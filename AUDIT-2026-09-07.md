# Chain Trader audit — 7 September 2026

The app is a Hyperliquid market monitor and trade planner. It scans eight markets, draws strategy levels, calculates position sizing, and tracks hypothetical price touches. It does not submit orders or verify actual fills.

## Problems fixed

| Finding | Effect before the fix | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rolling candle indexes changed zone and setup identity | Repeated signals, stale zone references, and distorted strength scores | Use candle timestamps for identity, rebase indexes, prune expired zones, and apply strength bonuses once |
| Historical scans used later target/HTF information | Targets could depend on information unavailable when a setup fired | Evaluate target zones and closed higher-timeframe context at each trigger |
| Old setups could be presented as newly actionable | Previously consumed entries/targets generated misleading alerts | Suppress setups already consumed by later candles or the current quote before creating new alerts |
| Cached prices could advance unrelated signals | Updating one market could process another market using an old price | Only evaluate lifecycle for fresh incoming quotes |
| Archived touched plans continued changing | Final timestamps or outcomes could change after closure | Keep closed records terminal |
| Accepted plans depended on current settings | Changing defaults silently changed displayed risk for old plans | Snapshot equity, risk percentage and leverage on acceptance |
| Network requests could hang | Loading and scanners could get stuck | Apply cancellable 15-second deadlines, retry initial loads, and retry failed background scans after 30 seconds |
| WebSocket status and recovery were unreliable | Acknowledgments could look like live data; failed/silent subscriptions stalled | Validate market/timeframe/OHLCV, heartbeat, detect missing candles, reconnect and detach old callbacks |
| API data was insufficiently validated | Malformed numbers or raw order-book objects reached consumers | Strict finite OHLCV and mid-price parsing; normalize order-book levels; reject empty verified history |
| Chart fast updates skipped corrected/rolling history | Displayed candles could disagree with refreshed data | Rebuild the series when historical bars change |
| Invalid risk inputs reached sizing | NaN, Infinity and out-of-range inputs caused misleading results | Validate bounds and return finite zero sizing for invalid inputs |
| Settings dialog let keyboard focus escape | Keyboard users could interact with the dashboard behind the dialog | Trap focus, close with Escape, restore focus and validate wallet format |
| Strategy record called gross results “Net R” | Results implied deductions that were never calculated | Label total R as gross and explain full-target assumptions |
| Outdated framework/dependencies | Known dependency advisories | Patch Next.js and compatible dependencies; audit the resulting lockfile |

## Risk Desk integration

The selected plan now offers **Open in Risk Desk**. The draft includes asset, direction, timeframe, exact entry/stop/target, equity, risk percentage and leverage. The receiving app validates values and direction, uses plan-specific sizing without overwriting the journal, preserves the strategy target, and blocks duplicate logging of a source plan. It applies its own fee model.

Published privately on 8 September 2026. The live site successfully imported the monitor’s exact XRP entry and target with no browser errors; the test draft was cleared without logging a live journal entry.

The handoff is one way and does not place orders. Wallet addresses and credentials are not included. Draft values are transported in a URL fragment, removed after import and retained for reload in that tab's session storage. Sign in to Risk Desk first; if authentication interrupts an import, return to the monitor and reopen the plan. Editing a draft or recording an outcome in Risk Desk does not update Chain Trader.

## Remaining limits and recommended next work

1. **Run the scanner continuously on a server.** The browser scanner stops when the tab closes or the computer sleeps; background tabs can throttle it. Move scanning and event storage to a persistent service before relying on unattended monitoring or overnight alerts.
2. **Reconcile with actual Hyperliquid account activity.** A public-address account feed could show open positions, orders and fills next to plans, with an explicit difference between inferred touches and confirmed fills. Current outcomes are hypothetical.
3. **Add durable shared history and backups.** Browser storage is device/origin specific and can be cleared. The two apps currently keep separate data; add authenticated storage if journal/monitor synchronization across devices is required.
4. **Validate the strategy prospectively and through replay.** Confidence is a heuristic score, not a measured win probability. Collect sufficient forward results and test fees, funding, slippage, partial exits and missed intra-poll touches before interpreting profitability.
5. **Unify fee and size-rounding rules.** The monitor sizes gross risk; Risk Desk can include fees in the budget. Review those quantities and exchange precision before entering orders manually.

No browser test can establish profitability, exchange fill quality, or reliable alerts while the computer sleeps. Audio/OS notification delivery and authenticated wallet balances were not exercised with a real account in this audit.
